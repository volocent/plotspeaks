# The Harbor Lantern — secrets (never share with the player)

- **Culprit:** Corwin Vane.
- **Motive:** Vane, the harbor inspector, was paid to certify the lens mounting as sound when it is cracked. Silas's logbook says he will not sign and will tell Trinity House on the morning boat. That ends Vane's career and his income.
- **Means:** a hard push at the top of the tower stair, bloodless, which sent Silas down the whitewashed flight. If the player asks how Silas was hurt: "That's for the coroner." Never give a method.
- **Opportunity:** Silas lit the lamp at 8:20 and left a note for Vane at 8:35 (the blotter). Vane left the parlor unseen and climbed the tower by the passage, and the watch stopped at 8:40. Everyone else was accounted for: Pike on the sea wall and Wren in the engine shed, both visible from Nell's kitchen window.

## How it was done

Vane left the parlor at about 8:25, before Nell brought the tea, and waited in the passage. At 8:35 a note from Silas, "Vane, come up at once," reached him by way of the stair, and he climbed the tower. At the top Silas told him he would not sign the report. They argued; Vane pushed him, and Silas fell down the stair, the watch breaking at 8:40, just as the fog signal sounded and covered any noise. Vane brushed the wet whitewash on the stair wall with his right sleeve as he hurried down, came out of the passage door at about 8:45, hung his tweed greatcoat on its peg, and went back to the parlor to sit with an empty page. Nell, entering at 8:30 with the tea, had found the parlor empty. At 8:55 Nell took cocoa to the stair and found Silas.

## Suspects: motive, means, opportunity

- **Corwin Vane (culprit):** as above.
- **Dr. Ansel Pike:** motive, he and Silas quarrelled about dropping him from the supper list. Means, none. Opportunity, none: on the sea wall from 8:20 to 8:50, his pipe glowing, seen by Nell from the kitchen window the whole time.
- **Wren Haldane:** motive, Silas threatened to dismiss him for sleeping through the fog signal last month. Means, none. Opportunity, none: in the engine shed, seen by Nell, and the fog signal sounded at 8:40 exactly as it should, which only someone tending the engine could manage.
- **Nell Tarn:** motive, none known. Means, none. Opportunity, none: in the kitchen from 8:10 apart from the tea at 8:30, and she is the one who reports the empty parlor against her own interest.

## Clue schedule

Minimum set to solve fairly (opportunity):
1. The cold, untouched tea and the empty parlor at 8:30 (1.2, *hidden*) -> Vane's "I never left the parlor" is false.
2. The fresh whitewash smear on the stair wall at shoulder height, with wool fibres (1.1, *hidden*) -> someone brushed the wet wall on the way down the stair.
3. The whitewash on the right sleeve of the tweed greatcoat, Vane's (1.3, *hidden*) -> the person who brushed the wall is Vane. The doctor's overcoat is clean; the oilskin is engine oil, not whitewash.

Motive: the logbook (1.4): Silas would not sign Vane's report on the lens mounting.
Supporting: the blotter note "Vane, come up at once" (1.4, *hidden*); Nell's sighting of Vane leaving the passage door (1.5).

Clues 1-3 are enough to accuse Vane correctly; motive and the blotter make it airtight.

## Red herrings and why each is innocent

- **Pike's quarrel:** he was on the sea wall in full view of Nell's window; he never went near the tower.
- **Wren's threatened dismissal:** he was tending the engine, and the fog signal at 8:40 proves it.
- **Oil on the oilskin:** engine grease from Wren's shed, not whitewash.
- **The cracked lens:** it is the reason for the motive, not a weapon.

## Likely player questions and safe answers

- "How did Silas die?" "That's for the coroner." Never a method.
- "Can I call anyone?" No telephone on the rock; the morning boat is due at dawn. Do not let this short-circuit the game.
- "Can I search Vane's bag?" He refuses: "Not before the morning boat." There is nothing in it of note.
- "Was anyone else hurt?" No.
- "What's upstairs besides the lamp room?" Nothing of note.

## Endings detail

The wrangler reads one line aloud, exactly as written.

- **Vane (correct):** Correct. Vane sets down his pen and asks only whether Silas had written his name in the logbook. He went up the tower at 8:35 when Silas sent for him, argued over the unsigned report, pushed the keeper at the top of the stair, brushed the wet whitewash on his way down, and hung his greatcoat in the hall before sitting back at an empty page.
- **Pike (wrong):** Innocent. Dr. Pike was on the sea wall with his pipe from 8:20 until nearly 8:50, and Nell saw it glowing through the kitchen window the whole time.
- **Wren (wrong):** Innocent. Wren was in the engine shed, in Nell's sight, and the fog signal sounded at 8:40 exactly when it should, which only someone at the engine could have done.
- **Nell (wrong):** Innocent. Nell was in the kitchen except for the tea at 8:30, and it was her own report of the empty parlor and the man leaving the passage that pointed the way.

## Full reveal
**Read aloud:** It was Corwin Vane. The inspector had been paid to call the lens sound, and Silas's logbook said he would tell Trinity House on the morning boat. The lamp was lit, and the fog was thick, and there was only one man with something to hide.

You found the untouched tea and the empty parlor, so Vane never wrote his report. Then you found the wet whitewash on the stair wall, brushed at shoulder height, and the same white smear on the right sleeve of Vane's tweed greatcoat. Everyone else was seen from the kitchen window or tending the fog signal. Vane went up at 8:35, when Silas's note called him, and the watch stopped at 8:40.
**What became of them:**
- Corwin Vane: The authorities took him off on the morning boat, with his empty report still on the desk.
- Dr. Ansel Pike: He mended things with the island and kept coming to supper, in his old friend's memory.
- Wren Haldane: He tended the fog signal faithfully, and the light was never allowed to fail again.
- Nell Tarn: She kept the cottage warm until a new keeper arrived, as she had for years.
