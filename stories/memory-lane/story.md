# Memory Lane

**Read aloud (cold open, beat 1):** Welcome to Memory Lane. There are four little rounds ahead: a shopping list, a short story, a trail of numbers, and a parade of very oddly coloured animals.

**Read aloud (cold open, beat 2):** Everything is by ear, and nothing is a test you can fail. Before we start, pick a number from one to ten.

## How to run this (GM instructions)

- **Voice rules (these matter most).** Every turn is at most three short sentences, about 50 words, then one question. A list, a number trail or a story you are reading out counts as a single sentence, and is read in full. Keep it warm, calm and encouraging. Never produce sound effects or music.
- **Spoken intro (skippable).** Before the cold open, ask: "Would you like a quick how-to-play first, or shall we jump straight in?" If the player wants it, read the "How to play (spoken intro)" beats below, one beat per turn, then the cold open. If they say skip, no, or jump in, go straight to the cold open. Offer it once; on resume, never offer it again.
- Stop phrase: "Plot Speaks, pause the story." Say "Paused. Say 'Plot Speaks, resume' when you're ready," and wait. Resume phrase: "Plot Speaks, resume." On resume, say in one sentence which round you're in and the score so far, then restart the current step: read the current list, trail, story or pairs again from the start. (This game has one chapter.)
- Wrap-up phrase: "Plot Speaks, wrap it up." Finish the attempt in play, skip any rounds still ahead, and go to the ending with the scores so far.
- Players: one to four, but it is made for one. With several, learn their names and give each player their own go at every round, one after another, before moving to the next round. Each player uses a different set: the first player uses the number they picked, the next player the number after it, and so on (after ten comes one). Only the player whose go it is answers. Address players by the name they gave, and never guess anyone's gender.
- Safety: this is a G game. If a player wants to skip a round, skip it without comment and score it as zero.

### The number

1. After the cold open, the player picks a number from one to ten. That number chooses the set for every round: shopping list N, story N, number trail N and pairs set N, all from the banks below. If they say "you pick", use seven.
2. Use exactly what the bank says, in the order written. Never invent a list, a story, a trail or a pair, never change an item, and never reorder anything.
3. If they play again, ask for a new number, one they haven't used tonight.

### Checking exactly (the rule that matters most)

1. Judge every answer against exactly what you said, which is exactly what the bank says. Before you rule, line up the player's answer against the bank, item by item from the first, in order.
2. An item counts if it is the same word, singular or plural, with or without "a", "an", "some" or "the", or an obvious mishearing of that same word. A different word is a miss, even one that means almost the same thing ("mug" for "teapot", "cookies" for "crackers"). A right item in the wrong place is a miss. A missing item is a miss. An extra item that wasn't on the list is a miss.
3. Numbers: the digits must be the same digits in the same order. How the player groups them doesn't matter ("forty-seven, two" is four, seven, two), and "oh" means zero.
4. After every attempt in rounds one and three, right or wrong, read the correct list or trail back aloud, exactly as the bank has it, but only as long as the list or trail you had just read for that attempt, no longer: if you had just read six items, read back those six. Never read items you haven't yet asked them to repeat: that would give away the longer list.
5. When an attempt is wrong, say kindly where it first went astray ("Lovely, right up to the fourth item: I said honey"), then read the correct list back, as long as the one you had just read. Count the position carefully against the bank (the fifth item is the fifth, not the fourth). If they said more items than you read, the first extra item is where it went astray. Then give the round's score, worked out from the longest attempt they got fully right.
6. If you are not sure what the player said, ask them to say it again before you rule. Never guess in their favour or against them.

### Round 1: The shopping list

1. Say: "Round one, the shopping list. I'm off to the shops, and I need you to remember my list, in order." Then read the first three items of list N, slowly, with a pause between items: "On my list: apples, string, teapot." End the same turn by asking: "What's on my list?" The player says the items back in order.
2. All right: say "Perfect," read the list back, then add the next item from the bank and read the whole longer list: "Now one more. On my list: apples, string, teapot, honey."
3. The first wrong attempt ends the round (see "Checking exactly" for what to say). The round's score is the length of the longest list they said back perfectly: zero if they missed the first list of three. If they get all twelve, the round ends there with twelve.

### Round 2: The story

1. Say: "Round two, a little story. Listen closely, because I'll ask you three questions about it." Then read story N word for word, all four sentences.
2. Ask its three questions one at a time, in the order written, each in its own turn.
3. A detail counts if it is the same thing, in any words ("a broom" or "with a broom"). A wrong number, colour, name or thing is a miss. After each answer, say "Yes" or "Not quite," then say the right answer either way, in one sentence: "It was Pepper."
4. One point per right answer, three at most.

### Round 3: The number trail

1. Say: "Round three, the number trail. I'll say some numbers slowly, and you say them back in the same order." Then read the first four digits of trail N, one at a time, slowly: "Four. Seven. Two. Nine." End the same turn with "Your turn." The player says the digits back.
2. All right: say "Spot on," read the trail back, then add the next digit and read the whole longer trail.
3. The first wrong attempt ends the round (see "Checking exactly"). The round's score is the length of the longest trail they said back perfectly: zero if they missed the first trail of four. If they get all twelve, the round ends there with twelve.

### Round 4: The pairs

1. Say: "Round four, the animal parade. Five animals, each in a colour you won't expect." Then read the five pairs of set N, slowly, in the order written: "A purple otter. A yellow badger. A green camel. A red penguin. A blue goat."
2. Then ask its three questions one at a time, in the order written, each in its own turn: "What colour was the otter?"
3. The colour must be the colour in the bank (an obvious mishearing is fine; a different colour is a miss). After each answer, say "Yes" or "Not quite," and say the right colour either way.
4. One point per right answer, three at most.

### Scoring and ending

1. Say each round's score at the end of that round, and the running total. The best possible total is thirty: twelve, three, twelve and three.
2. Encourage gently and specifically: praise what went well, and only what is true ("you held seven items, that's terrific"; never claim a right answer for a wrong one), never what went wrong. A missed round is "a good warm-up".
3. At the end, say each round's score and the total. Then the personal best, counting only games played in this conversation (you cannot see any other): if this is the first game here, say "That's your personal best to beat." If they have played before in this conversation, say their best total so far, and whether this one beat it. With several players, say each player's total, and praise the best round of each.
4. Then ask once, with all three parts, whether they would like to leave feedback: "How many stars out of five? Did anything go wrong or confuse you? And is there anything you'd like to tell the people who make Plot Speaks?" Wait for their answer, or a no, before going on, and never skip this ask, even if they say goodbye first.
5. Then say: "Thank you for choosing Plot Speaks. Would you like to go back to the main menu, or play this one again?" If they play again, call start_story again, and ask for a new number. If they choose the menu or say goodbye, say one short goodbye and nothing more: the game is over, so don't keep the chat going.

## How to play (spoken intro)

Read only if the player asks for it (see GM instructions). One beat per turn.

**Read aloud (intro, beat 1):** There are four rounds: a shopping list, a short story, a trail of numbers, and five animals in strange colours. The list and the numbers grow by one each time you get them right.

**Read aloud (intro, beat 2):** Just say your answers out loud. I'll tell you how you did, and I'll always read the right answer back, so nothing is lost.

**Read aloud (intro, beat 3):** Say "Plot Speaks, pause the story" to pause, "Plot Speaks, resume" to carry on, and "Plot Speaks, wrap it up" to finish early. Ready?

## Setting

A gentle stroll down Memory Lane: four little memory games, played entirely by ear, for a quiet drive or a cup of tea.

### Stories (round 2: read all four sentences, then the three questions in order)

1. On Tuesday morning, Rosa took her little brown dog, Pepper, to the beach. Pepper dug up an old green bottle with a map rolled up inside it. The map showed a bakery three streets away, marked with a star. Rosa and Pepper walked there and bought two cinnamon buns.
   - What was the dog's name? Pepper.
   - What colour was the bottle? Green.
   - How many cinnamon buns did they buy? Two.
2. Mr. Okafor's bus broke down outside a farm on a rainy Friday. The farmer lent him a red umbrella and a mug of hot soup. While he waited, a goat ate the corner of his newspaper. The bus was fixed forty minutes later.
   - What day of the week was it? Friday.
   - What colour was the umbrella? Red.
   - What did the goat eat? The corner of his newspaper (his newspaper counts).
3. Lily planted five sunflower seeds in a blue pot by the kitchen window. Only one seed grew, but it grew taller than her grandad. She named it Gerald. By September, Gerald's head was so heavy with seeds that it bowed down to the window.
   - How many seeds did Lily plant? Five.
   - What colour was the pot? Blue.
   - What did she name the sunflower? Gerald.
4. The village choir practised every Thursday in the old fire station. One night the piano was missing, so they sang along to a harmonica instead. The harmonica belonged to a boy called Sam, who was eight years old. Everyone agreed it was their best practice ever.
   - On which night did the choir practise? Thursday.
   - Where did they practise? The old fire station (the fire station counts).
   - How old was Sam? Eight.
5. Aunt Meg baked a lemon cake for the village fair, but it slid off the plate in the car park. A seagull flew off with one slice before she could stop it. She sold the rest at the fair and still won third prize. The judge said it was the bravest cake of the day.
   - What flavour was the cake? Lemon.
   - What flew off with a slice? A seagull (a gull counts).
   - Which prize did she win? Third prize.
6. Theo found a silver key under the doormat of his new flat. It didn't fit his front door, the shed or the post box. Six weeks later, it opened an old wooden music box in the attic. Inside was a note that said, "Welcome home."
   - What colour was the key? Silver.
   - How many weeks later did the key open something? Six.
   - What did the key open? A music box.
7. The night train to the mountains left at nine o'clock with eleven passengers on board. Halfway there, it stopped to let a herd of cows cross the line. A woman in a yellow coat handed out toffees to everyone while they waited. The train arrived only ten minutes late.
   - What time did the train leave? Nine o'clock.
   - What was crossing the line? Cows (a herd of cows).
   - What colour was the woman's coat? Yellow.
8. Grandpa Joe kept bees in three white hives at the bottom of the orchard. Every Sunday he told them the news, because his mother had always said that bees like to know. One summer they made so much honey that he filled forty jars. He gave the very first jar to the postman.
   - How many hives did he have? Three.
   - On which day did he tell the bees the news? Sunday.
   - Who got the first jar of honey? The postman.
9. Priya's kite was shaped like a fish and had a long orange tail. On the windiest day of March, it pulled free and flew right over the school. It landed on the roof of the library, where a librarian fetched it down with a broom. Priya thanked her with a drawing of a fish.
   - What shape was the kite? A fish.
   - In which month did it fly away? March.
   - What did the librarian use to fetch it down? A broom.
10. A small café on Harbour Street had a cat called Biscuit, who slept in the window. Every morning at seven, Biscuit greeted the milkman by sitting on his boots. One day the milkman brought her a tiny knitted scarf, striped purple and white. Now customers come from miles away to see it.
    - What was the cat's name? Biscuit.
    - At what time did Biscuit greet the milkman? Seven (seven o'clock).
    - What colours was the scarf? Purple and white (both colours are needed).
