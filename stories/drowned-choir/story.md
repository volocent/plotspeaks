# The Drowned Choir

**Read aloud (cold open, beat 1):** Scarth, a northern fishing village, Monday 5 October 1953. After last night's harvest festival at St Ebba's chapel, Walter Kell the choirmaster was found at low water, drowned below the sea wall.

**Read aloud (cold open, beat 2):** Walter wrote asking you here: "There is something I mean to put right, and I'd like a steady person beside me." Last night he pressed your hand: "Tomorrow I'll tell you why." Are you his relative, the new curate, or a retired police inspector?

**Read aloud (cold open, beat 3):** Nan Bell of the Ship Inn woke you at half past seven. "They're saying he slipped," she says, "and Scarth will say it forever." Walter lies in the lifeboat house on the quay; where do you begin?

## Setting

Scarth, a grey stone fishing village on the northern coast, Monday 5 October 1953, the morning after the harvest festival. Cobles drawn up on the slipway, gulls on the chapel roof, the smell of tar and wet slate, and out past the harbour mouth the Mells, a black reef that shows only at low water. Twenty-five years ago a herring drifter was lost there on the night before harvest, and Scarth does not talk about it.

## Chapter 2 — The Providence (≈33 min)

### Scene 2.0 — Ada Hume's kitchen (opens Chapter 2)
**Read aloud:** Ada Hume's kitchen window looks out past the harbour mouth to the Mells, where the ebb is uncovering a line of black iron ribs. "That's the Providence," she says. "Twenty-five years, and she still comes up at the low tides."
**Available:** question Ada; the newspaper cutting she keeps in her Bible; the photograph on the dresser; the view of the Mells and the quay.
**Ada says freely:** the Providence was running home in a sudden gale on harvest Saturday, 6 October 1928, seven men aboard, five of them Walter's choirmen hurrying back for the festival. "She struck the Mells at a quarter to nine. Adam knew those rocks blind; he'd have come in on the pier light." The lifeboat brought none of them home. The inquiry blamed Adam: "They said he'd taken drink at Holmness. He never touched it." At the supper last night Walter told her: "By next Sunday, Adam's name is clean." He wouldn't say how. If asked about last night: "Hester Kell sat with me from twenty to ten till past eleven. Walter sent her; he said I'd want company this week." She doesn't know where Walter was on the night of the wreck, or what he saw.
**Clues here:**
- The cutting, from the Alnbury Gazette, November 1928: "The keeper, Mr. Edward Arkle, and his son Thomas, who assisted him, swore that the pier light was lit at a quarter past six, as entered in the log. Miss Margaret Shaw, who was walking on the quay, said she saw it burning at seven. The court found the vessel was lost through an error of judgement by her skipper." Ada doesn't know who Margaret Shaw was; "some lass off the quay."
- The photograph: seven men on the Providence's deck in 1928. Ada names Adam, at the wheelhouse door.
- The view: the Mells and the wreck's ribs; the quay, the harbour office and the lifeboat house.
**Chapter 2 turn:** in Chapter 2, if the player has opened the organ bench and been to one other Chapter 2 scene, or has been to three Chapter 2 scenes, play Scene 2.5 as they leave here.
**Leads to:** 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 2.4

### Scene 2.1 — The organ loft
**Read aloud:** The organ loft is narrow and dusty, the stops worn pale by Walter's hands. Its one small window looks straight down the length of the sea wall to the pier light. The organ bench has a brass lock.
**Available:** the window; the organ; the organ bench (the small brass key from Walter's ring opens it; if the player hasn't got it, the vicar brings it up); question the vicar, waiting at the foot of the stair.
**Clues here:**
- The window: the whole wall, the shelter, the pier light, and the Mells beyond.
- *hidden* (player opens the organ bench): under the music, two papers.
  - A draft letter in Walter's tall sloping hand, first part: "To H.M. Coroner, Alnbury. On the night of 6 October 1928 I was at this organ, and from the loft window I saw the pier light dark at twenty to nine. At twenty-five past nine I saw the keeper's lad run out along the wall and light it, after the Providence had struck. Skipper Adam Hume was not to blame."
  - The draft, the rest: "Ned Arkle gave me the true page of his log before he died in 1931, and I will bring it to you. I have kept this silence twenty-five years, and it is mine to answer for. I will tell him first, to his face, and if he will stand up in chapel and say it himself, I will stand beside him."
  - A yellowed page torn from a log book, in pencil, in a careful young hand: "Sat. 6 Oct. 1928. Wind rising SE. Lit 9.25 pm. T.A."

### Scene 2.2 — The harbour office
**Read aloud:** The harbour office is one room on the quay: a stove, a chart of the coast, a shelf of old ledgers. Tom Arkle sits writing at the high desk and doesn't get up. "Harbour business," he says; "what is it?"
**Available:** question Tom; this year's light log on the desk; the shelf of old light logs back to 1910; the stove; the peg by the door with his oilskin.
**Tom says freely:** the same as on the wall. If asked about 1928: "I was twenty. My father kept the light. The inquiry said what it said." If asked about his reefer jacket: "At home."
**Clues here:**
- This year's log, in pencil, a small square hand: "Sun. 4 Oct. Lit 6.05 pm. T.A." Every night's entry is signed "T.A."
- The 1928 log (if the player asks for it or looks along the shelf): "Sat. 6 Oct. Lit 6.15 pm. E.A." squeezed in at the top of a page in a heavier pencil; just before it, the ragged stub of a page torn out.
- The stove: cold now, full of ash. Tom: "It's my stove."
- *hidden* (player rakes the ash or looks in the stove's ash pan): the charred corner of a long envelope, a few words left in a tall sloping hand in black ink: "...oner, Alnbury."

### Scene 2.3 — The Arkles' kitchen
**Read aloud:** The Arkles' kitchen on the quay is warm and spotless, the kettle on the range. Peggy Arkle has been crying and is pretending she hasn't. A clothes horse stands before the range with a man's jacket hung on it.

**Clues here:**

### Scene 3.0 — The vestry (opens Chapter 3)
**Read aloud:** The vestry is small and cold, surplices on their hooks, the harvest loaves going stale on a tray. The vicar shuts the door and stands with his back to it. "I didn't walk straight home last night," he says.
**Available:** question the vicar; the surplices; the window over the harbour. The vicar stays here when the player leaves; elsewhere, he can't answer.

## Endings

- **The verdict.** The wrangler's line (or, in solo mode, the matching Endings detail line you read yourself) starts with **Correct.** or **Innocent.** Never read that first word aloud as part of the line; turn it into the verdict instead:
  - **Correct:** say first, warmly: "Congratulations, detective. You've solved it." Then the rest of the line, then the correct-accusation ending below.
  - **Innocent:** say first, gently: "I'm sorry. You've accused an innocent person." Then the rest of the line, then the wrong-accusation ending below.
- **Correct accusation:** read aloud: "The seven o'clock bus stops at the head of the slipway, and Sergeant Craddock walks out along the wall to the light. Tom Arkle takes the key of the pier light off its string and puts it in Ada Hume's hand. Would you like to hear what became of each of them?"
- **Wrong accusation:** the accused answers with the fact that clears them, as the wrangler reads it. Then, in that same turn, the next unfound lead in this chapter's "Keeping it moving" is given by the person named (always, unless every lead is found). Then ask: "Do you want one more accusation, or the full reveal?"
- **Out of time** (the player says "Plot Speaks, wrap it up" or asks to wrap up, or hasn't named anyone by the end of their second turn after Scene 3.2): read the latest recap block, with only what the player has found, then: "The seven o'clock bus is coming down the hill, and Ada Hume is waiting. If you had to name someone right now, who would it be?" Resolve as above.
